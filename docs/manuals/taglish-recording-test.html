Lend us your Taglish

We are building voice screening for applicants. Before it goes live we have to choose which transcription engine to use — and the only way to choose honestly is to test it on people who talk the way our applicants actually talk. That is you. It takes about ten minutes.

When Tomorrow, 8 September Who 3–5 people, Back Office or HR Time ~10 minutes each

What we are building

An applicant fills in the form, and then — if they want to — answers seven questions by voice, from their own phone, whenever they like. No appointment, nothing to attend. HR listens and records the first-round decision in one tap.

That part already works. The next step is to transcribe the answers automatically, so HR reads instead of listens. Ten minutes of audio per applicant becomes something you can skim in under a minute, search, and paste into a note.

Nothing about the hiring decision changes. The transcript is there so a person can get through more applicants without skipping any.

Why we need your voice specifically

We have already compared two engines on public recordings. One result stopped us.

What the person said

I started as service crew for two years, tapos nag-apply ako sa kitchen kasi gusto kong matuto mag-prep.

What the engine wrote

I started as service crew for two years. tapos nag-apply ako sa kitchen kasi gusto kong matuto mag-prep

It dropped half the answer — and what is left still reads as a complete, sensible sentence. Nobody reading the transcript would know anything was missing.

Those numbers come from public read-aloud recordings, where each clip is in one language only. Real applicants switch inside a sentence, which is the exact thing we are worried about and the exact thing that data cannot show. That is the gap your recordings close.

What we are asking you to do

  1. 1

    Open the real application form on your phone

    sushizen-shift-pwa.vercel.app/apply

    Please do not use a voice memo app. The application page records in the exact audio format production uses (Opus, 24 kbps, through the browser). A voice memo is a different format, and a measurement taken on it would not predict what happens on a real applicant.

  2. 2

    Put STT Test 1 as your name

    Test 1, Test 2, Test 3 — whichever number is yours. This is how we find these records again and delete them. Your phone number can be your real one; nobody will call it.

  3. 3

    Answer the seven questions the way you normally talk

    This is the one that matters. Switch between English and Tagalog in the middle of your sentences, the way you would talking to a colleague. Please do not try to answer in one language — a clean English answer or a clean Tagalog answer repeats a test we have already run and tells us nothing new.

  4. 4

    Sit somewhere quiet

    Background noise is the biggest risk we found — both engines fall apart in it. We want to measure the language problem, not the noise problem, so give it a quiet room.

  5. 5

    Tell us when you are done

    We will then write down by hand what you actually said, word for word, and compare it against what each engine produced. There is no answer key for real speech — a person has to make one.

What happens to your recording

Used for
Comparing how accurately each transcription engine writes down mixed English and Tagalog. Nothing else.
Who hears it
The people running the comparison. It is not shared with your manager and it is not part of any review.
Deleted
As soon as the comparison is finished — the audio and the STT Test application record together. This is a promise about the test recordings specifically, not the ordinary applicant retention rule.
Not an application
You are not applying for anything. Nothing you say is scored, kept on your file, or looked at again.
Optional
You can say no, and you can stop halfway. We need three people; if more than five volunteer we will not use all of them.

What happens after

We run your recordings through both engines with the same measurements as the first comparison, and check whether the ranking holds. If it does, we fix the engine and build the transcript into the HR screen. If it does not — if whisper-1 turns out to drop words on real Taglish the way the other engine did on the public clips — then we have found that out on five colleagues instead of on five hundred applicants.

Either way we will tell you what your recordings showed.

Questions about the recording itself, or if the page will not use your microphone: ask before you start rather than working around it — a recording made a different way cannot be used.
